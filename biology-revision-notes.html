1. Human Nephron & Urine Formation
Nephron Structure: Functional unit of kidney. Composed of:
1. Bowman's capsule: Cup-shaped sac enclosing glomerulus.
2. Glomerulus: Capillary tuft where high-pressure blood filtration occurs.
3. Renal tubule: PCT (Proximal Convoluted Tubule), Loop of Henle (descending & ascending limbs), DCT (Distal Convoluted Tubule) & Collecting Duct.
Mechanism of Urine Formation:
1. Glomerular Filtration: Blood filtered across glomerular capillary membrane into Bowman's space (filters water, glucose, salts, urea; retains blood cells & proteins).
2. Selective Reabsorption: ~99% filtrate reabsorbed in PCT & loop of Henle (glucose, amino acids actively; water, Na+, Cl- passively/actively).
3. Tubular Secretion: Active secretion of H+, K+, NH3, and creatinine from peritubular capillaries into tubule to maintain ionic & pH balance.
4. Concentration: Loop of Henle & vasa recta (counter-current system) and collecting duct conserve water under ADH control.
2. Neuron & Synaptic Transmission
Neuron: Structural & functional unit of nervous system; excitable cell that conducts impulses.
Synaptic Transmission (Chemical Synapse):
1. Arrival of Impulse: Action potential reaches presynaptic knob/terminal.
2. Ca²⁺ Influx: Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open; Ca²⁺ enters presynaptic bulb.
3. Neurotransmitter Release: Ca²⁺ triggers synaptic vesicles to fuse with presynaptic membrane; releases neurotransmitters (e.g., Acetylcholine) into synaptic cleft by exocytosis.
4. Receptor Binding: Neurotransmitters diffuse across cleft and bind specific chemo-receptors on postsynaptic membrane.
5. New Impulse: Ion channels open (Na+ influx), generating post-synaptic potential. If threshold is reached, a new action potential fires.
3. Calvin Cycle (C₃ Cycle)
Light-independent CO₂ reduction in chloroplast stroma. Discovered by Melvin Calvin.
1. Carboxylation: CO₂ combines with RuBP (5C) catalyzed by RuBisCO → unstable 6C intermediate splits into 2 molecules of 3-PGA (3C).
2. Reduction: 3-PGA converted to G3P/PGAL using 2 ATP + 2 NADPH per CO₂. Some G3P synthesizes glucose/carbohydrates.
3. Regeneration: Remaining G3P regenerates RuBP using 1 ATP.
Net Input for 1 Glucose (6 turns): 6 CO₂ + 18 ATP + 12 NADPH → 1 Glucose + 18 ADP + 12 NADP⁺.
4. Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation (5 Marks)
Definition: ATP formation from ADP + Pi using light energy where electron flow is unidirectional from H₂O to NADP⁺.
1. PS II (P680): Absorbs 680 nm light; electrons excited and expelled.
2. Photolysis of Water: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂ catalyzed by oxygen-evolving complex (Mn²⁺, Cl⁻) in PS II. Supplies replacement e⁻.
3. ETC & ATP: Electrons pass through Pheophytin → Plastoquinone (PQ) → Cytochrome b₆f → Plastocyanin (PC). Energy drives ATP synthesis via proton gradient.
4. PS I (P700): Light excites P700; transfers e⁻ through A₀ → Fe-S → Ferredoxin (Fd).
5. NADPH Formation: Fd-NADP+ reductase transfers e⁻ to NADP⁺ + H⁺ → NADPH.
Products: ATP, NADPH, and O₂.
5. Krebs Cycle (TCA / Citric Acid Cycle)
Site: Mitochondrial matrix. Completely oxidizes Acetyl-CoA (2C).
1. Citrate Formation: Acetyl-CoA (2C) + Oxaloacetate (OAA, 4C) + H₂O → Citric Acid (6C) + CoA (enzyme: Citrate synthase).
2. Isocitrate to α-Ketoglutarate (5C): Oxidative decarboxylation produces 1 CO₂ + 1 NADH.
3. Succinyl-CoA (4C): α-Ketoglutarate undergoes oxidative decarboxylation → 1 CO₂ + 1 NADH.
4. Succinate: Succinyl-CoA → Succinate + 1 GTP (yields 1 ATP).
5. Fumarate & Malate: Succinate → Fumarate (produces 1 FADH₂) → Malate (+H₂O).
6. OAA Regeneration: Malate → Oxaloacetate (produces 1 NADH).
Net Gain per Acetyl-CoA: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP/GTP, 2 CO₂. (×2 per Glucose molecule).
6. Hatch & Slack Pathway (C₄ Cycle)
Alternative CO₂ fixation in tropical plants (Maize, Sugarcane) with Kranz anatomy.
Mesophyll: CO₂ + PEP (3C) --[PEP carboxylase]--> OAA (4C)
→ Malate (4C) → transported to Bundle Sheath Cells
Bundle Sheath: Malate → Decarboxylation → CO₂ (enters Calvin Cycle) + Pyruvate (3C)
→ Pyruvate transported back to Mesophyll to regenerate PEP.
Significance: Eliminates photorespiration, operates at high temps/light, high water-use efficiency.
7. Glycolysis (EMP Pathway) - 5 Marks
Site: Cytoplasm. Universal breakdown of 1 Glucose (6C) → 2 Pyruvate (3C). Anaerobic.
Why it occurs: Generates immediate cellular ATP, produces NADH, and provides pyruvate for aerobic respiration.
Glucose (6C) --[ATP→ADP]--> Glucose-6-P → Fructose-6-P --[ATP→ADP]--> Fructose-1,6-bisP (6C)
→ Cleaves into: DHAP (3C) ↔ Glyceraldehyde-3-P (PGAL, 3C)
→ 2 PGAL --[+2NAD⁺ → 2NADH]--> 2 × 1,3-bisPGA
→ 2 × 3-PGA (+2 ATP) → 2 × 2-PGA → 2 × PEP → 2 × Pyruvate (+2 ATP).
Net Yield: 2 Pyruvate + 2 ATP (net) + 2 NADH.

8. Anatomy of Dicot vs Monocot Stem
Dicot Stem (Sunflower): 1. Epidermis with multicellular hairs. 2. Hypodermis is collenchymatous. 3. Ground tissue differentiated into cortex, endodermis, pericycle & pith. 4. Vascular bundles arranged in a ring, open (cambium present), wedge-shaped. 5. Medullary rays present. 6. Large central pith.
Monocot Stem (Maize): 1. Epidermis with thick cuticle, trichomes absent. 2. Hypodermis is sclerenchymatous. 3. Ground tissue undifferentiated (no distinct cortex/pith). 4. Vascular bundles scattered, closed (no cambium), smaller towards periphery. 5. Sclerenchymatous bundle sheath present. 6. Protoxylem lacuna present.
9. Anatomy of Dicot vs Monocot Root (5M)
Dicot Root (Pea/Sunflower): 1. Epiblema with unicellular root hairs. 2. Cortex of thin parenchymal cells. 3. Endodermis with Casparian strips. 4. Pericycle gives rise to lateral roots & vascular cambium. 5. Vascular bundles 2–6 (diarch to hexarch), alternate. 6. Pith small or absent. 7. Secondary growth present.
Monocot Root (Maize/Wheat): 1. Epiblema with root hairs. 2. Broad parenchymatous cortex. 3. Endodermis with Casparian strips. 4. Pericycle gives rise to lateral roots only. 5. Polyarch (numerous xylem bundles >6). 6. Pith large and well-developed. 7. Cambium absent (no secondary growth).
10. Cellular Development Phases
Differentiation: Meristematic cells undergo maturation, lose division ability, and acquire specific structure & function. (e.g., Formation of xylem tracheary elements, phloem sieve tubes).
De-differentiation: Living mature permanent cells regain the capacity of cell division under specific conditions. (e.g., Formation of interfascicular cambium & cork cambium/phellogen from parenchyma).
Redifferentiation: Cells produced by dedifferentiation mature again, lose division ability, and specialize. (e.g., Formation of secondary xylem, secondary phloem, and cork/phellem).
11. Anatomy of Dicot vs Monocot Leaf
Dicot Leaf (Dorsiventral - Sunflower): 1. Upper epidermis with cuticle; lower epidermis with more stomata (hypostomatic). 2. Mesophyll differentiated into upper Palisade (photosynthesis) and lower Spongy parenchyma (gas exchange). 3. Reticulate venation; vascular bundles varied in size. 4. Bulliform cells absent.
Monocot Leaf (Isobilateral - Maize): 1. Similar upper and lower epidermis; stomata equal on both sides (amphistomatic). 2. Mesophyll undifferentiated (all uniform photosynthetic cells). 3. Parallel venation; vascular bundles uniform size. 4. Bulliform cells present in upper epidermis (regulate rolling/unrolling during drought).
12. Flower Structure & Solanaceae Family (5M)
Flower: Reproductive shoot with 4 whorls: Calyx (sepals, protective), Corolla (petals, attracts pollinators), Androecium (male, stamens with anther/filament), Gynoecium (female, carpels with stigma/style/ovary).
Solanaceae (Potato, Tomato, Brinjal, Datura): Inflorescence: Solitary/cymose. Flower: Bisexual, actinomorphic, pentamerous. Calyx: (5) persistent, valvate. Corolla: (5) gamopetalous, valvate. Androecium: 5, epipetalous. Gynoecium: Bicarpellary, syncarpous, superior ovary, bilocular, axile placentation with swollen placenta. Fruit: Berry/capsule. Seeds: Endospermic.
13. Bryophyte & Pteridophyte Life Cycles (5M)
Bryophytes (Mosses, Liverworts): Alternation of generations. 1. Gametophyte: Dominant, haploid (n), green, free-living. 2. Sex organs: Antheridia (male) & Archegonia (female). 3. Water required for biflagellate sperm motility → Zygote (2n). 4. Sporophyte: Diploid (2n), physically dependent on gametophyte; capsule produces haploid spores by meiosis.
Pteridophytes (Ferns): 1. Sporophyte: Dominant, diploid (2n), independent with true root, stem, leaves. 2. Sporangia produce haploid spores (n) via meiosis. 3. Spores germinate into small, independent, photosynthetic, heart-shaped Prothallus (gametophyte, n). 4. Water needed for fertilization → Zygote → new sporophyte.
14. Enzyme Inhibition: Competitive vs Non-Competitive
Competitive Inhibition: Inhibitor closely resembles substrate in structure and competes for the active site of the enzyme. Vmax remains same, Km increases. Reversible by increasing substrate concentration. Example: Malonate competes with succinate for Succinate dehydrogenase.
Non-Competitive Inhibition: Inhibitor binds to an allosteric site (other than active site), altering enzyme conformation. Vmax decreases, Km remains unchanged. Cannot be reversed by adding substrate. Example: Cyanide inhibits Cytochrome oxidase; Heavy metals (Hg²⁺, Pb²⁺, Ag⁺) inhibit enzymes.
15. Respiratory Volumes & Capacities (5 Marks)
1. Volumes:
• TV (Tidal Volume): Normal tidal breathing volume = ~500 mL.
• IRV (Inspiratory Reserve): Forcible extra inhalation = 2500–3000 mL.
• ERV (Expiratory Reserve): Forcible extra exhalation = 1000–1100 mL.
• RV (Residual Volume): Volume remaining in lungs after max exhalation = 1100–1200 mL.
2. Capacities:
• IC (Inspiratory Capacity): TV + IRV (3000–3500 mL).
• EC (Expiratory Capacity): TV + ERV (1500–1600 mL).
• FRC (Functional Residual): ERV + RV (2100–2300 mL).
• VC (Vital Capacity): ERV + TV + IRV (3500–4500 mL).
• TLC (Total Lung Capacity): RV + ERV + TV + IRV = VC + RV (5000–6000 mL).
